Exhibit A: a spoof, replayed

An illustrative order book with made-up numbers, the kind of pattern I reviewed every day. Scroll, or drive it yourself.

Index futures, front month Last 5,000.50
BidsPriceOffers
Alert: possible spoofing, Trader X 900 lots offered, 0 filled, all cancelled 380 ms after a 20-lot buy. Order-to-trade ratio 45 to 1.

  1. A quiet book

    Buyers on the left, sellers on the right, a few dozen lots at each price. Nothing unusual.

  2. The layers go in

    One trader adds 900 lots to sell, a few ticks above the market, where they are unlikely to trade.

  3. The market leans

    Other traders see a wall of sellers and shade their prices down. The best offer drops to 5,000.25.

  4. The real trade

    The same trader buys 20 lots at the lower price. That buy was the goal.

  5. The layers vanish

    380 milliseconds later, all 900 lots are cancelled. The wall disappears and the price recovers.

  6. The alert, then the work

    Surveillance links the cancels to the buy. Then the work starts: rebuild the day, test for intent across sessions, and write a memo that holds up in front of enforcement.

What I look for

The same handful of schemes, in every pit since the wheat traders of 1920. Each one leaves a pattern in orders, cancels and fills.

  • Spoofing

    - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

    Big orders, one side
    Small fill, other side
    Cancelled in milliseconds
    Tell: no intent to trade

  • Layering

    - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

    Orders stacked at
    several price levels
    Same trader, same side
    Pulled after the fill

  • Wash trading

    - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

    Same owner, both sides
    No change in position
    Prints fake volume
    Tell: linked accounts

  • Marking the close

    - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

    Orders bunched in
    the closing window
    Aim: the settlement price

  • Front-running

    - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

    Trades just ahead of
    a known customer order
    Same direction
    Profits from its impact

  • Pre-arranged

    - - - - - - - - - - - - - -

    Both sides agreed
    before the order hits
    Skips open competition

A note from Rahul

I got into surveillance because markets leave a trail. Every order, cancel and fill is on the record. The job is reading that trail well enough to tell intent from noise, then writing it up so it holds under scrutiny.

At Cboe I did that across options and futures with SMARTS, Python and SQL. At FINRA I learned the other side: how an obligation in a rulebook becomes a control someone actually runs. At Polymarket I build both into the systems of a CFTC-regulated exchange and clearinghouse.

Outside work, soccer taught me to read movement under pressure. Origami taught me precision. The ukulele is still teaching me patience; I wrote an app so I could learn it by ear.
