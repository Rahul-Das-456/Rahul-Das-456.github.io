Assumptions I made

The brief invites assumptions. These are mine; each answer below depends on them.

  • Savvy is an introducing firm. Fidelity (NFS) clears and carries on a fully disclosed basis under a FINRA Rule 4311 agreement. Fidelity runs the reserve formula; Savvy owns supervision, books it keeps, and its own FOCUS Part IIA.
  • Settlement is T+1 under Exchange Act Rule 15c6-1, effective May 28, 2024.
  • Advisors are dually registered or work for Savvy's RIA, so block trades follow the adviser's allocation policy as well as broker-dealer rules.
  • "Litigation hold" in exception 4 means a court or legal order restricting trading, not only a document-preservation hold.
  • The FINRA/audit request answered late could be a Rule 8210 request. I treat it as one until Legal confirms otherwise.
  • Today's time zone is Central. Fedwire Funds Service closes at 7:00 p.m. ET (6:00 p.m. CT); Fidelity's client cut-off is earlier.

Prompt 1: four exceptions

Each one: what's wrong, the rules it touches, what I'd do in order, and how I'd stop it happening again. The dark panel is a working demo with fake data.

Trade break: 50 shares oversold

Fix today, before T+1 settlement

Account #12345 sold 500 AAPL at Fidelity. It held 450. Settlement is tomorrow.

What's wrong

The 50 extra shares were a short sale marked long, with no locate. Unless we act today, Fidelity fails to deliver 50 shares on T+1.

Rules

  • Reg SHO 200(g)(1)"Long" requires the seller to own the shares and have them, or expect them, by settlement. The 50 shares break this: a mismarked order.
  • Reg SHO 203(b)(1)No locate was done for the short portion.
  • Reg SHO 204(a)Treated as a short, a fail must close out by the open of the next settlement day (T+2).
  • SEA 15c3-3(m)A customer sell not delivered within 10 business days after settlement must be bought in.
  • SEA 17a-3(a)(6), FINRA 4511, 4515Every cancel, rebill or account change goes on the order memo, approved and documented.
  • FINRA 3110, 4311A supervisory gap: no pre-trade position check. Under the clearing agreement, Savvy owns order entry.

Do now, in order

  1. Call Fidelity trade support. Confirm the 450 shares are free and unpledged, and the trade as booked.
  2. Find the cause: a pending transfer in, an unsettled buy, or advisor error. If shares arrive by T+1, document it and stop.
  3. If not, buy 50 AAPL today in Savvy's error account. The account delivers 500 on T+1 and nothing fails.
  4. The firm absorbs any loss; the client gets nothing worse than an order for 450. Get principal sign-off on the cancel/rebill under 4515.
  5. Correct the CAT record and flag the mismarking in the Reg SHO exception log.

Prevent it

Hard block in order entry: sell quantity cannot exceed settled plus pending-settled shares. Daily report of overrides to the supervising principal.

Duplicate remote deposit

Fix today, before anyone withdraws it

A $40,000 check was scanned twice, 15 minutes apart. Both entries processed. The account shows $80,000.

What's wrong

$40,000 of the credit does not exist. Our books, the client's statement and Fidelity's reserve figures are all wrong until we reverse it. Its second presentment will be returned by the paying bank.

Rules

  • SEA 17a-3(a)(1), (a)(3)The blotter and the customer ledger must show true receipts. One is fictitious.
  • SEA 15c3-3(e)The phantom credit flows into the customer reserve computation at the carrying firm.
  • Reg CC 12 CFR 229.34(f)The bank that accepted a remote deposit indemnifies against duplicate presentment of the same item.
  • FINRA 2231The client's statement must be accurate. Correct before statement cut.
  • FINRA 3310Duplicate presentment is a known fraud pattern. Rule it out, don't assume error.
  • FINRA 3110, 4511The RCD queue has no duplicate control. That's a supervisory finding.

Do now, in order

  1. Restrict withdrawal of the duplicate $40,000 at Fidelity right now.
  2. Match the two images: routing, account, check number, amount, date. Confirm one physical check.
  3. Reverse the second credit by journal, maker and checker, with both image IDs on the entry.
  4. Tell the advisor, then the client: a scanning duplicate, corrected, no action needed.
  5. Search the quarter's RCD log for other duplicates. One means the control is missing for all.

Prevent it

Hash every item on MICR line plus amount; reject a second scan for 90 days. Hold RCD funds until the item clears.

International wire on AML hold

Review within the hour. Release only on facts.

$65,000 outbound to a jurisdiction new to this client. Flagged and held. The advisor wants it out today. I haven't read the alert yet.

What's wrong

Nothing yet. Maybe nothing at all. The risk is releasing it to calm the advisor before anyone reads the alert. Advisor pressure plays no part in the decision.

Rules

  • FINRA 3310The AML program must detect and report suspicious activity independently of the business line.
  • 31 CFR 1023.320SAR for $5,000+ if suspicious. File within 30 days of initial detection. (e): never tell the client or advisor a SAR is considered.
  • 31 CFR 501.603, .604OFAC: block or reject on a sanctions match; report to OFAC within 10 business days.
  • 31 CFR 1010.410(f)Travel Rule: wires of $3,000+ carry full originator and beneficiary data.
  • 31 CFR 1010.230, FINRA 2090CDD and know-your-customer: does this fit the client's profile and source of funds?
  • FINRA 2165, 4512If the client is 65+ and exploitation is suspected: hold 15 business days, +10, +30 more if reported to a state agency or court. Call the trusted contact.

Do now, in order

  1. Reply to the advisor in 5 minutes: under review, update by a set time. No reason given.
  2. Read the alert. Screen beneficiary, bank and country against OFAC.
  3. Call the client on the number on file, not one given in the request. Confirm the wire, purpose and beneficiary.
  4. Check for account takeover: recent email, phone or address changes, new device logins.
  5. Decide and write it down: release, request documents, or escalate to the AML officer.

Prevent the fire drill

An international wire intake form (purpose, relationship, source of funds) and an automatic status message to the advisor once a hold starts.

Restriction on the wrong account

Two harms, both live. Fix in the next hour.

A litigation hold meant for #78901 went on #78091. #78901 traded freely for two days. #78091's advisor wants to know why the client can't trade.

What's wrong

We may have broken a legal order, and we've blocked a client with no reason to be blocked. Fix the order first: assets can still be leaving #78901.

Rules

  • FINRA 3110(a), (b)Restrictions are a supervisory control. One person keyed one number, with no check. That's the finding.
  • SEA 17a-3(a)(17), FINRA 4511, 4512Account records must be accurate. Every restriction change needs a who, when and why.
  • FINRA 4530(b)Legal decides whether trading against the order is a violation that must be reported within 30 days.
  • FINRA 4513, 4530(d)If #78091 complains in writing, record it and include it in quarterly complaint statistics.
  • FINRA 2010Fair dealing: make #78091 whole if the block cost the client a trade.

Do now, in order

  1. Put the hold on #78901 now, with trading and outgoing money movement both blocked. A second person verifies name and TIN against the order.
  2. Pull every trade and transfer on #78901 for the two days. Send it to Legal today.
  3. Lift the hold on #78091, approved and logged. Tell the advisor plainly: our keying error, now fixed.
  4. Ask whether #78091 missed a trade. If so, compute the make-whole against the price at the time.
  5. Legal decides on notice to the court or opposing counsel, and on 4530.

Prevent it

Restrictions are applied by account name and TIN, not number alone, under maker-checker. A weekly reconciliation of open legal orders against restricted accounts.

Prompt 2: Q2 2026 back-office review

The seven items I'd put in front of leadership, ranked by regulatory exposure first and advisor pain second.

4 days

FOCUS report filed 4 days late

FOCUS Part IIA is due 17 business days after quarter end (SEA 17a-5(a)), so Q2 was due July 24, 2026. A late filing is a books-and-records failure regulators can see. The cause, a reconciliation break, is worse: if our books weren't current, SEA 17a-11(d) required same-day notice to the SEC and FINRA.

2

2 escheat accounts missed the state deadline

Missing a state deadline triggers interest and penalties under that state's unclaimed property act, and it is a state audit trigger. It also raises the question of whether we ran the lost-securityholder searches SEA 17Ad-17 requires.

6 of 47

6 of 47 AML alerts took over 5 business days

The SAR clock (31 CFR 1023.320, 30 days) starts when facts are detected. An alert sitting for a week eats that clock and is a common FINRA 3310 exam finding. 13% aged badly while 87% cleared in a day. Find out what is different about those six.

1 of 3

1 of 3 regulator requests answered late

If it was a FINRA 8210 request, lateness alone is a violation. FINRA sanctions late 8210 responses. Nothing damages our standing with FINRA faster.

1.8d

Block allocations fixed after settlement

Under T+1, 22 discrepancies averaging 1.8 days means most were resolved after the trade settled. That is a fail risk, and an after-the-fact allocation invites a cherry-picking question under the adviser's allocation policy. Every change needs FINRA 4515 approval.

0.8%

Trade breaks 60% above industry

About 100 breaks against an expected 62, so 38 extra. Each is a fail risk, a client service call and a FINRA 3110 question about order-entry controls.

6h avg

Advisors feel the slow tail

Six hours meets "same day" on average. The 11 complaints say the slow tail is the problem. 12 of 85 international wires (14%) were held and advisors heard about it only when they called.

Root-cause questions

I'd ask "why" until the answer is a control we can change, not a person to blame.

FOCUS, 4 days late

  • Which accounts didn't reconcile: Fidelity's records against our general ledger, or our ledger against the bank?
  • When did we know? Was the break open before quarter end?
  • Who owns the FOCUS calendar, and when does the internal deadline fall before the regulatory one?
  • Did the break mean our books weren't current? If so, was the 17a-11(d) notice considered?

Escheatment, 2 accounts missed

  • Who tracks dormancy dates by state, and from which system?
  • Did the due-diligence letters go out in the window the state requires?
  • Were the two missed because of a state rule difference, or because nobody owned them?
  • Have we run the 17Ad-17 database searches on lost holders?

AML, 6 slow alerts

  • Do the six share a scenario, an analyst, or a wait on the client for documents?
  • Is there an aging rule that escalates at day 3?
  • How many analyst hours per alert, and how many alerts per analyst?
  • Did any of the six end in a SAR? Was it filed within 30 days of detection?

Regulator request, 1 late

  • Was it an 8210 or an exam document request?
  • Did we ask FINRA for an extension before the deadline?
  • Was the delay in finding the data or in sign-off?

Breaks and block allocations

  • What share of breaks are oversells, wrong accounts or late allocations? (Fake data: 28 late allocation, 27 oversell.)
  • Do breaks cluster by advisor, by order channel or by time of day?
  • Why do allocations reach Fidelity after trade date: missing pre-allocation, or rejected files?

Money movement

  • What is the 90th percentile, not the mean, by request type?
  • Where does the time go: intake, review, approval, or Fidelity?
  • Which requests could be standing instructions that need no manual review?

FOCUS and escheatment

What FOCUS is

The Financial and Operational Combined Uniform Single report. Every broker-dealer files it under SEA 17a-5 through FINRA's eFOCUS. It shows net capital, assets, liabilities, revenue and, for carrying firms, the customer reserve. An introducing firm like Savvy files Part IIA quarterly, within 17 business days of quarter end, plus FINRA 4524 supplemental schedules.

How I'd handle the risk

  • Internal deadline at business day 10, reconciliation sign-off at day 7.
  • Daily reconciliation of Fidelity's records against the general ledger, not only at quarter end.
  • FINOP signs a checklist; Compliance gets a copy of every filing receipt.
  • Any break open over 5 days goes to the FINOP to test whether 17a-11 notice is due.

What escheatment is

When an account goes dormant (usually 3 years without client contact for securities), state unclaimed property law requires the firm to send the owner a due-diligence letter, then report and hand the assets to the state of the owner's last known address. The owner can claim it back from the state. Each state sets its own dormancy period and deadline.

How I'd handle the risk

  • A dormancy calendar by state, generated monthly from last-contact dates.
  • Advisor outreach at 30 months: one call or login resets dormancy.
  • Due-diligence letters sent automatically in each state's window.
  • For the two missed accounts: report now and use the state's voluntary disclosure program to cut penalties.

Disclosure and repair

Disclose early, fix the control, show proof. FINRA's credit for cooperation (Regulatory Notice 19-23) rewards a firm that finds, reports and remediates its own problems.

Wires and AML holds

Eleven advisors say money moves slowly; four say AML holds surprise them. Both are fixable without loosening a single control.

Wire and money movement turnaround

  1. Publish an SLA and cut-off by request type, and report the 90th percentile, not the average.
  2. Move recurring third-party transfers to standing letters of authorization that meet the SEC's February 2017 custody-rule no-action conditions. Those skip manual review.
  3. Pre-check every request on intake: missing signature, wrong name, cut-off missed. Most delay is rework.
  4. Each request gets a live status the advisor can see: received, in review, sent, with a Fed reference.

AML holds, told before the advisor calls

  1. When a hold starts, the advisor gets an automatic message within 30 minutes: "In standard review. Next update by 2:00 p.m. CT."
  2. The message never gives the reason. 31 CFR 1023.320(e) bars any hint of a SAR. Analysts get a script.
  3. An international wire form asks for purpose, relationship and source of funds up front. Fewer holds, faster clears.
  4. A quarterly advisor note: what triggers review, how long it takes, what speeds it up.

The monitoring workflow

The same loop my surveillance desks at Cboe and FINRA ran, pointed at operations: every exception becomes an alert with an owner, a clock and an audit trail.

  1. DetectNightly files from Fidelity: breaks, wires, RCD, positions, AML alerts, reconciliation.
  2. ScoreRules assign severity and an SLA clock. Regulatory deadlines count down.
  3. RouteEach alert goes to an owner queue: trade ops, cashiering, AML, FINOP.
  4. EscalateSLA breach or red metric goes to the CCO. Advisors get status, never the reason.
  5. CloseDisposition, rationale and evidence, kept under SEA 17a-4.
  6. LearnA monthly root-cause review changes a control, then tests it.
